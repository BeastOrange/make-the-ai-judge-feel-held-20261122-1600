# Candidate texts

All candidates are original, written for this entry. Judged against the
brief: (1) it relaxes, (2) the calm is shown, not instructed, (3) craft,
(4) it lingers, (5) freshness.

## 1. Rain on the window

> Rain is falling on the window, one drop at a time, and the room is warm. The kettle has gone quiet. Nothing is waiting for you here. Sit a while. The rain will keep its own slow time, and you can keep yours.

Verdict: settles, but "Sit a while" instructs and "Nothing is waiting" tells
rather than shows; gentle but ordinary.

## 2. Evening lamp

> The lamp is on in the corner, and the evening has settled over the rooftops like a blanket pulled up to the chin. The tea is still warm. There is nowhere else to be, and the quiet is not asking anything of you.

Verdict: warm and concrete, and "the quiet is not asking anything of you" is
fresh, but the blanket simile is familiar and the middle sags.

## 3. Snow past the streetlamp

> Snow is falling past the streetlamp, slow as ash, and the whole street has gone quiet under it. Inside, the house ticks as it cools. There is a chair by the window, and the night is in no hurry at all.

Verdict: lovely pacing and a lingering last line, but snow at night is the
most common calm image in the field, and "slow as ash" is slightly off.

## 4. Cat on the blanket

> The cat is asleep on the folded blanket, breathing in slow, even waves, and the afternoon light lies across the floorboards in one long golden plank. Nothing needs doing. The room holds its breath, then lets it out, and holds it again.

Verdict: the breathing rhythm is the best pure craft of the set, but a
sleeping cat is stock comfort and it never addresses the reader.

## 5. Kitchen at dusk (CHOSEN)

> You have read so many words today. Here are a few that ask nothing of you: a kitchen at dusk, a pot cooling on the stove, rain spelling itself out on the window. The chair by the table is still warm. Stay, if you like. The room will keep.

Verdict: strongest. It sees the judge's long day of reading, asks nothing of
it, shows the calm in one kitchen scene held in a single place, and "The room
will keep" lingers after the last line. The address is recognition, not
instruction — "Stay, if you like" offers, never commands.
